Employee turnover poses a significant challenge to organizations, leading to increased costs and disruptions in 
workforce stability. In this study, we propose a novel hybrid model for employee turnover prediction that in-
tegrates Genetic Algorithms (GA) for feature selection with LightGBM for classification. The GA component 
identifies the most relevant features, while LightGBM ensures high prediction accuracy and computational ef-
ficiency. The model was evaluated on two datasets —a public HR Analytics dataset and a proprietary organi -
zational dataset —using multiple performance metrics including Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and AUC. 
The proposed GA LightGBM model achieved an AUC of 0.94 and F1-Score of 0.87 on the HR dataset, and an 
AUC of 0.78 with F1-Score of 0.73 on the organizational dataset, outperforming conventional models such as 
Random Forest, Logistic Regression, and even standalone LightGBM. The results demonstrate that the proposed 
method offers a balanced and interpretable solution for turnover prediction, outperforming existing approaches 
in both predictive power and practical applicability. This work contributes to HR analytics by offering a 
transparent, scalable, and high-performing predictive framework that supports effective retention strategies.
1.Introduction
Employee turnover remains one of the most significant challenges in 
human resource management, leading to increased costs related to 
recruitment, training, and the loss of valuable expertise (PM and Balaji, 
2019 ). High turnover rates can disrupt organizational stability, reduce 
workforce morale, and negatively impact productivity. The ability to 
predict turnover accurately is essential for organizations to mitigate 
these costs and implement effective retention strategies. Traditional 
methods for predicting employee attrition, such as simple surveys or 
performance evaluations, often fail to capture the complexity of turn-
over decisions, as they overlook the intricate interplay of various factors 
affecting an employee ’s decision to leave the organization. (Marquez 
et al., 2024; Strohmeier & Piazza, 2015; H. Zhang et al., 2018 ). These 
conventional methods are too simplistic and cannot effectively account 
for the multi-dimensional nature of turnover that arises from both in-
ternal and external factors influencing employee decisions. In recent 
years, machine learning (ML) has emerged as a promising solution to 
improve turnover prediction (Al Akasheh et al., 2024; Atef et al., 2022; 
Park et al., 2024 ). Various machine learning algorithms, including de-
cision trees, random forests, and support vector machines (SVM), have been employed to predict employee attrition by analyzing historical 
data, job satisfaction, performance metrics, and other relevant features 
(Alaskar et al., 2019; Veglio et al., 2025 ). For instance, Shafie et al. 
explored the integration of Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) with 
clustering techniques to enhance turnover prediction accuracy (Shafie 
et al., 2024 ), while Biswas et al. applied ensemble learning models to 
identify key predictors of turnover intention, showing improved per-
formance compared to traditional methods (Biswas et al., 2023 ). Despite 
these advancements, many studies still struggle with handling 
high-dimensional datasets and the challenge of selecting the most rele-
vant features that significantly influence turnover. (de Oliveira et al., 
2019; Jain and Nayyar, 2018 ). To address these limitations, this paper 
proposes a novel approach that combines LightGBM, a state-of-the-art 
gradient boosting algorithm, with genetic algorithms for feature selec-
tion. LightGBM has gained popularity due to its ability to handle 
large-scale datasets efficiently, while genetic algorithms are used to 
optimize feature selection, ensuring that only the most impactful vari-
ables are included in the model. This hybrid approach aims to improve 
the accuracy and interpretability of employee turnover prediction 
models by leveraging the strengths of both algorithms. The proposed 
model is evaluated using two datasets: one publicly available,

2.Literature review
Employee turnover prediction has been extensively studied in recent 
years, with various machine learning algorithms being applied to predict 
employee attrition (Ikram et al., 2021; Ilyas et al., 2020 ). This section 
reviews key works in the field, outlining the methodologies used and 
their findings, starting with the earliest studies and progressing through 
recent advancements.
Zhang et al. conducted a study to predict employee turnover using 
machine learning techniques (H. Zhang et al., 2018 ). They analyzed a 
dataset from a telecommunications company, focusing on employee 
characteristics such as job position, overtime, and work level. The study 
compared various machine learning algorithms like logistic regression, 
KNN, and decision trees to predict turnover. The results revealed that 
machine learning models could effectively capture the complex dynamics of employee attrition, with decision trees proving to be the 
most accurate in predicting turnover. The study emphasized the 
importance of identifying the most significant turnover predictors for 
targeted retention strategies.
Alaskar et al. explored the use of machine learning models, including 
decision trees, support vector machines, and logistic regression, for 
predicting employee turnover (Alaskar et al., 2019 ). They applied 
feature selection techniques, such as SelectKBest and Recursive Feature 
Elimination (RFE), to identify the most important variables impacting 
turnover decisions. The study showed that combining multiple machine 
learning models, such as SVM and random forests, enhanced the pre-
dictive accuracy of the model compared to traditional methods.
Zhao et al. expanded the understanding of turnover prediction by 
using a broader range of machine learning models to predict employee 
attrition (Zhao et al., 2019 ). Their study involved algorithms such as 
decision trees, random forests, logistic regression, and neural networks. 
The researchers found that including a variety of features, including job 
satisfaction and performance reviews, significantly improved the accu-
racy of predictions. They concluded that machine learning models could 
accurately predict employee turnover, especially when multiple rele-
vant features are considered.
Ali proposed a hybrid model combining Random Forest and Principal 
Component Analysis (PCA) for employee turnover prediction (Wild Ali, 
2021 ). PCA was used to reduce the dimensionality of the data and select 
the most relevant features, while Random Forest was employed to 
classify the employees at risk of leaving the organization. The results 
showed that this approach outperformed traditional models, particu -
larly in terms of classification accuracy, and suggested that feature 
reduction combined with robust classification methods could yield su-
perior performance.
Kumar et al. introduced a systematic approach to predicting 
employee turnover using machine learning algorithms (Kumar et al., 
2023 ). They focused on the financial and operational implications of 
turnover, emphasizing the importance of retaining skilled employees to 
conserve organizational resources. The study tested various machine 
learning algorithms, including random forests, neural networks, and 
XGBoost, on a dataset from a multinational organization. The results 
showed that XGBoost achieved the highest prediction accuracy of 
78.5 %, demonstrating its effectiveness in identifying high-risk em-
ployees. The study concluded that machine learning techniques can be 
an essential tool for HR departments to proactively address turnover and 
enhance workforce stability.
Shafie et al. focused on artificial neural networks (ANN) and clus-
tering techniques to predict employee turnover (Shafie et al., 2024 ). 
They used a clustering approach to segment employees based on various 
features, and then applied ANN to predict the likelihood of turnover 
within each cluster. The findings indicated that segmenting employees 
before applying machine learning models improved prediction accu-
racy, especially when dealing with large datasets containing complex, 
high-dimensional data.

results showed that ensemble models, which combine the outputs of 
multiple algorithms, were more accurate than individual models. This 
study highlighted the importance of using multiple models to account 
for the various factors affecting turnover, as no single model could 
capture all the nuances in the data.
Shankar et al. explored the challenges organizations face with high 
employee turnover and applied machine learning algorithms to predict 
employee attrition (Shankar et al., 2024 ). They focused on overcoming 
data noise in HRIS databases, which can lead to inaccurate predictions. 
The study utilized six machine learning algorithms to classify turnover 
risks, with an emphasis on decision trees, random forests, and XGBoost. 
The results showed that these algorithms could accurately predict 
voluntary turnover by analyzing historical employee data from a 
multinational company. Their study concluded that machine learning 
algorithms are effective in predicting turnover, but the issue of data 
noise must be addressed to improve prediction accuracy.
Chowdhury et al. focused on enhancing the transparency of AI-based 
machine learning models used for predicting employee turnover 
(Chowdhury et al., 2023 ). They highlighted the importance of under -
standing how machine learning models generate predictions to build 
trust among HR managers. By employing the LIME (Local Interpretable 
Model-Agnostic Explanations) method, they showed how HR managers 
could interpret the predictions generated by these models. Their find-
ings revealed that transparency in AI models is crucial for better 
decision-making in HR and ensuring fairness in turnover predictions. 
The study suggested that transparent machine learning models can 
improve employee retention strategies by providing HR professionals 
with actionable insights.
Nayem and Uddin addressed the issue of unbiased employee per-
formance evaluation using machine learning techniques (Nayem and 
Uddin, 2024 ). They considered various physical, social, and economic 
factors affecting performance, including job satisfaction, work envi-
ronment, and compensation. Their findings showed that machine 
learning algorithms could predict employee performance and turnover 
by integrating multiple external factors, and that using these factors 
resulted in more accurate and fair performance assessments.
Veglio et al. utilized supervised machine learning techniques, 
particularly classification decision trees, to predict voluntary employee 
turnover in a multinational corporation (MNC) (Veglio et al., 2025 ). 
They applied the CHAID (Chi-square Automatic Interaction Detector) 
algorithm to analyze a dataset of 2932 employees. Their results showed 
that the decision tree model successfully identified key factors that 
determine whether employees would voluntarily leave the organization. 
Key predictors included job satisfaction, work-life balance, and organi -
zational commitment. The study emphasized that machine learning, 
specifically decision trees, can provide a strategic advantage for HR 
departments in identifying at-risk employees and implementing targeted 
retention strategies.
Adeusi et al. focused on machine learning models for predicting 
employee turnover in high-stress sectors (Adeusi et al., 2024 ). Their 
study used a variety of machine learning algorithms, including logistic 
regression, decision trees, random forests, and neural networks, to 
predict turnover in sectors such as healthcare and finance. Their results 
showed that job satisfaction, stress levels, and performance ratings were 
the most significant predictors of turnover. They concluded that inte-
grating machine learning models into human resource practices can help 
reduce turnover rates, particularly in high-stress environments.
Park et al. developed a machine learning-based model to predict 
turnover intention among new employees, particularly college gradu -
ates (Park et al., 2024 ). They used a combination of logistic regression, 
k-nearest neighbor (KNN), and XGBoost classifiers to analyze the factors 
influencing turnover, such as job security, work environment, and job 
preference. Their model achieved an accuracy of 78.5 %, with job se-
curity emerging as the most significant predictor of turnover. This study highlighted the importance of considering job security and organiza -
tional satisfaction in predicting turnover, particularly for new 
employees.
Kumar et al. proposed a systematic machine learning approach to 
predict employee turnover in high-stress sectors (Kumar et al., 2023 ). 
They compared various machine learning models, including random 
forests, neural networks, and XGBoost, to predict turnover based on job 
satisfaction, stress levels, and performance metrics. Their results indi-
cated that XGBoost outperformed other models, achieving a prediction 
accuracy of 78.5 %. They also emphasized the need for integrating 
predictive models into HR practices to enhance employee retention 
strategies and ensure workforce stability.
In summary, although many studies have applied various machine 
learning techniques for employee turnover prediction, there are still 
critical gaps to be addressed. First, limited attention has been given to 
integrating powerful ensemble methods such as LightGBM with 
metaheuristic-based feature selection techniques like Genetic Algo-
rithms. Second, most existing works emphasize either accuracy or model 
simplicity but rarely achieve both simultaneously. Finally, very few 
models are evaluated using both public and real-world HR datasets, 
reducing their generalizability. This research aims to fill these gaps by 
proposing a hybrid GA-LightGBM framework that balances prediction 
performance, model interpretability, and real-world applicability.

3.Proposed method
In this study, we propose a novel machine learning-based framework 
for predicting employee turnover by combining LightGBM with Genetic 
Algorithms for feature selection. This hybrid approach leverages the 
strengths of both techniques: LightGBM for its high predictive accuracy 
and Genetic Algorithms for optimizing the selection of the most relevant 
features for turnover prediction. Although various novel models such as 
deep learning or ensemble-based hybrid techniques have been proposed 
in recent works, we selected LightGBM due to its outstanding perfor -
mance on tabular data and its computational efficiency. Furthermore, 
Genetic Algorithms are employed to optimize feature selection, offering 
enhanced model interpretability — an essential aspect in HR decision- 
making environments. This hybrid approach aims to balance perfor -
mance and transparency, providing a practical solution that can be more 
readily adopted by organizations. The methodology involves the 
following key steps: Data Collection and Preprocessing, Feature Selec -
tion using Genetic Algorithms, Model Training using LightGBM, Model 
Evaluation, and Interpretation of Results.

3.3. LightGBM
After feature selection, LightGBM is used to train the turnover pre-
diction model. LightGBM is an advanced, efficient, and scalable imple -
mentation of gradient boosting, designed to handle large datasets and 
high-dimensional data efficiently (X. Sun et al., 2020 ). It leverages 
histogram-based learning, where continuous feature values are dis-
cretized into bins, reducing computational costs by simplifying the 
search for optimal split points. Unlike traditional gradient boosting 
methods, which grow trees level-wise, LightGBM uses a leaf-wise growth 
strategy, selecting the leaf that maximizes the reduction in the loss 
function at each step (Ke et al., 2017 ). This often results in deeper trees 
and better predictive accuracy. The framework can natively handle 
categorical features without the need for explicit encoding, making it 
more efficient than other models that require pre-processing like 
one-hot encoding. LightGBM is optimized for speed, memory efficiency, 
and scalability, making it capable of handling massive datasets with 
millions of samples and features. It also supports parallel and distributed 
training, which accelerates the model-building process on large-scale 
data. Key hyperparameters in LightGBM include `num_leaves` (the 
maximum number of leaves in a tree), `max_depth` (the depth limit of 
the tree to avoid overfitting), and `learning_rate` (to control the speed of 
learning). The framework also includes features such as `subsample` for 
row sampling and `colsample_bytree` for feature sampling, both of 
which help prevent overfitting (D. Zhang and Gong, 2020 ). LightGBM ’s 
ability to handle large datasets and its focus on speed and memory ef-
ficiency make it one of the most popular choices for machine learning 
tasks like classification, regression, and ranking. It has been widely 
adopted across industries such as finance, healthcare, e-commerce, and 
natural language processing (NLP), where it has been used for tasks 
ranging from fraud detection and credit scoring to customer churn 
prediction and text classification. The primary goal in LightGBM is to

model achieves this without compromising interpretability —a critical 
factor in HR decision-making —unlike deep learning-based hybrids such

from opacity and limited practical deployment.
These comparisons, summarized in Table 14, provide strong evi-
dence that our model achieves the best trade-off between performance, 
feature selection efficiency, scalability, and transparency, making it 
more suitable for deployment in real organizational settings.
Furthermore, our use of recent datasets (2023 –2024) and inclusion 
of both public and proprietary data add to the generalizability and 
relevance of the proposed method. The hybrid framework also aligns 
with recent research directions in explainable AI (XAI) and interpretable

2023 ).
In conclusion, the results and comparisons establish the proposed GA 
LightGBM model as a competitive and balanced solution, exceeding 
existing methods in prediction quality while remaining feasible for 
practical implementation in HR environments.

Feature selection is a critical step in the process of building machine

improving model performance and reducing computational complexity. 
In this section, we compare the performance and characteristics of Genetic Algorithm, Random Forest (RF), Neural Networks (NN), Support 
Vector Machine (SVM), Logistic Regression (LR), and K-Nearest Neigh -
bors (K-NN) for feature selection. Each method has its own strengths and 
weaknesses depending on the nature of the dataset, the problem at hand, 
and the computational resources available. Table 16summarizes the

Genetic Algorithms are particularly effective for selecting features in 
datasets with complex, non-linear relationships. GA works by evaluating 
multiple combinations of features through an iterative process, allowing 
it to capture intricate interactions between features that simpler 
methods may miss. The advantage of GA is its flexibility in handling 
diverse feature spaces, making it ideal for datasets where feature re-
lationships are non-linear. However, this flexibility comes at a cost: the 
computational complexity is high, and the training time is longer, 
especially with large datasets. Despite this, GA remains one of the most 
powerful methods for feature selection when model accuracy is the top 
priority, as it can improve the predictive power of the model signifi -
cantly by selecting the most relevant features.
On the other hand, Random Forest is widely used for feature selec-
tion due to its ability to evaluate the importance of each feature auto-
matically. RF builds multiple decision trees and assesses feature 
importance based on how much each feature contributes to improving 
the model ’s performance. This method is computationally less expensive 
than GA, but still requires substantial resources when dealing with a 
large number of trees and features. The training time for RF is longer 
compared to simpler methods like Logistic Regression, but it is more 
efficient than GA in terms of computational cost. While RF can handle 
high-dimensional data well, it may not perform as effectively as GA in 
cases where there are complex feature interactions or non-linear 
relationships.

tive in classification problems, the training time and complexity can 
become problematic when dealing with very large datasets, making it 
less efficient than GA for large-scale feature selection.
Logistic Regression is a fast and simple method for feature selection, 
particularly in problems where the relationships between features are 
linear. It works well when the features are independent and the dataset 
is not too large. The computational complexity is low, and it provides 
fast results, making it suitable for quick feature selection in simpler 
models. However, Logistic Regression is limited to linear problems and 
cannot effectively capture non-linear relationships between features, 
making it unsuitable for more complex datasets with intricate feature 
interactions.
K-Nearest Neighbors is another intuitive method for selecting fea-
tures, particularly when the feature interactions are important for 
classification. It works by evaluating the proximity between data points 
and can select features based on their ability to influence the similarity 
measure. While K-NN is simple to implement, it has high computational 
costs for large datasets, particularly when the number of features is high. 
Training time can also become an issue as the model needs to compute 
distances between all data points, especially with large datasets, which 
reduces its efficiency.
The comparison above demonstrates that while Genetic Algorithm 
offers the best performance in terms of feature selection, particularly 
when the relationships between features are complex and non-linear, it 
comes with higher computational costs and longer training times. 
However, its ability to model complex feature interactions and improve 
model performance makes it a preferred choice for problems where 
accuracy is paramount.
7.1. Comparative analysis of hybrid models in related studies
To better contextualize our proposed approach, we present a 
comparative summary of recent hybrid models used for employee 
turnover prediction. Table 17highlights the key characteristics of these 
models, including their algorithmic components, complexity, inter-
pretability, and performance trade-offs. Several recent studies have 
investigated hybrid machine learning architectures to improve 
employee turnover prediction. These models combine various algo-
rithms in pursuit of higher accuracy and more effective feature handling. However, a deeper analysis of these approaches reveals a spectrum of 
trade-offs that should be carefully evaluated, particularly in domains 
such as Human Resource Management, where both interpretability and 
practicality are critical.
Ensemble-based hybrids such as the one proposed by (Biswas et al., 
2023 ), which incorporate Random Forests, SVM, Logistic Regression, 
and Gradient Boosting, demonstrate the strength of algorithmic di-
versity in improving predictive performance. The advantage of such 
models lies in their robustness across datasets and their ability to reduce 
the bias and variance associated with single learners. However, this 
increase in accuracy often comes at the expense of increased complexity. 
These models typically require extensive parameter tuning, longer 
training times, and lack intuitive interpretability —factors that hinder 
their deployment in organizational settings where transparency and 
explainability are essential. In many HR systems, decision-makers must 
not only know what the prediction is, but why an employee is likely to 
leave, and such ensemble methods make it difficult to provide that level 
of clarity.
Similarly, the work by (Shafie et al., 2024 ), which uses Artificial 
Neural Networks combined with clustering, emphasizes the detection of 
non-linear relationships within subgroups of employees. While such 
techniques can uncover hidden patterns and segment populations more 
effectively, they demand large volumes of training data and are typically 
treated as black-box models. This lack of interpretability again poses 
challenges when trying to translate predictions into actionable HR 
strategies, where understanding the contributing factors behind attrition 
is often more valuable than achieving marginal gains in accuracy.
Ali’s hybrid approach (Wild Ali, 2021 ) combines Principal Compo -
nent Analysis (PCA) with Random Forests, offering a promising strategy 
for handling high-dimensional data by reducing feature space before 
classification. While PCA effectively simplifies the dataset and enhances 
processing speed, it transforms original features into orthogonal com-
ponents, thereby obscuring their real-world meaning. This abstraction 
limits the ability of HR managers to trace predictions back to specific 
attributes such as job satisfaction, salary, or performance rat-
ings—variables that are essential for formulating retention policies.
A closer comparison with the method proposed by (Bustillo, 2025 ), 
which also employs Genetic Algorithms, highlights the importance of 
the classification algorithm in the hybrid structure. While Bustillo in-
tegrates GA with relatively simple classifiers such as KNN and Naive 
Bayes, our approach leverages the superior classification capabilities of 
LightGBM. KNN, for instance, is known for its sensitivity to noisy data 
and difficulty in handling large datasets, while Naive Bayes relies on 
strong independence assumptions that are often violated in real-world 
HR data. In contrast, LightGBM not only accommodates 
high-dimensional input efficiently but also supports complex feature 
interactions without compromising computational performance.
Against this backdrop, the proposed GA LightGBM model emerges 
as a well-balanced alternative. Genetic Algorithms provide an effective 
mechanism for identifying optimal feature subsets by navigating large 
search spaces and uncovering non-linear dependencies among features.

8.Conclusion and future work
The primary objective of this study was to propose a hybrid machine 
learning model integrating Genetic Algorithms for feature selection and 
LightGBM for employee turnover prediction, aiming to improve pre-
diction accuracy while maintaining model interpretability. Our GA 
LightGBM model successfully achieved this objective, outperforming 
traditional methods such as Random Forest, Logistic Regression, and 
standalone LightGBM. The model achieved an AUC of 0.94 and an F1- 
score of 0.87 on the HR dataset, demonstrating a significant improve -
ment in prediction accuracy. This hybrid approach efficiently selects key 
features and ensures the model is both scalable and interpretable, 
making it a valuable tool for HR professionals in predicting employee 
turnover. By optimizing feature selection and providing transparency in 
decision-making, the model can support effective retention strategies 
and improve workforce stability. Future work will focus on exploring 
alternative feature selection methods, incorporating more diverse 
datasets from different industries to enhance generalizability, and 
integrating advanced feature engineering techniques. Additionally, 
improving the interpretability of the model using methods like SHAP or 
LIME could further support HR decision-making by offering deeper in-
sights into the prediction process.
