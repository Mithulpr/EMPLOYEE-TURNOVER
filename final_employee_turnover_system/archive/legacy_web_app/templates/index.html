Section 8 — Evaluation Protocol & Research Notes
Details

How to Interpret These Comparisons

  1. Published base-paper results use the original paper's evaluation dataset: Talebi et al. (2025) reported metrics on an external HR Analytics dataset (N = 2,932) with synthetic resampling, which limits direct numerical equivalence.
  2. Our base-paper reimplementation uses our own dataset: We evaluated the exact GA + LightGBM pipeline under 10-fold cross-validation on our 2,969 unique profiles, providing a genuine, reproducible baseline.
  3. Champion results come from recorded historical experiments: Every champion in our Top 5 table reflects a verified, archived milestone evaluated under documented project protocols.
  4. The official Step 32 evaluation uses a 594-record holdout: The legacy holdout partition was examined in earlier research iterations to confirm architecture viability.
  5. Cross-dataset differences are descriptive: Results from different splits or datasets are not directly comparable and do not prove superiority on external populations.
  6. Higher accuracy alone is insufficient: In imbalanced employee turnover (16% positive rate), high accuracy can be achieved trivially by predicting stayers. The true hallmark of our champion is its balanced 87.96% F1-score and 87.50% recall.
  7. Model outputs are probabilistic signals, not certainties: Predictions describe estimated turnover probabilities based on historical patterns, not deterministic individual employee actions.

Research Limitations & Ethical Governance

  • Retrospective Holdout Notice: Because the 594-record holdout was previously examined during candidate selection, external validation on fresh enterprise cohorts is necessary for production deployment.
  • Generalizability: The dataset is derived from an enterprise corporate cohort; generalization to other organizational cultures, industries, or compensation structures requires local calibration.
  • Observational Causality: SHAP feature attribution indicates mathematical contributions to model predictions and does not prove that altering an attribute will causally prevent turnover.
  • Prohibition of Adverse Actions: Model predictions must strictly serve as decision-support indicators for supportive HR outreach and must never trigger automatic termination, demotion, or compensation penalties.
